1. The sentence decides the tolerance

Elbow extension is the joint a coach corrects most. The engine can only say something a joint is further off than its tolerance — so the tolerance decides which sentences exist. Drag it.

2. Did hand landmarks rescue the elbow? No.

Five estimators on identical frames. Coverage went up 6.5×; the angle did not move. An estimator that ignores an improvement to its worst landmark was never limited by it.

5. Which numbers to trust: the estimator-swap band

Each joint is scored twice, by two pose models reading the same frames. The gap between the two reported deviations is the band — a lower bound on measurement uncertainty, not two takes of one athlete. Off-the-shelf: one extra pose pass, no new data.

6. What this does not claim

  • Not SOTA. 8-class head-to-head is dataset separability, not model strength (1-NN and single-frame controls in the report).
  • Camera side is not solved. Cross-view deviation is camera geometry; the card labels it instead of blaming the athlete.
  • The 18° tolerance is a measurement tolerance, not a fault threshold. It is bracketed by the within-take repeat spread (elbow 6.5–8.0° sd) and the estimator swap, so it is defensible for "the same movement read twice". Across 94 athletes the same elbow varies with sd 32.7°, so the card separates each athlete's own offset from the residual, and the effective gate is max(tolerance, 2.5×measured floor) — 21° for the right elbow.
  • No public dataset carries expert fault labels. Every dataset we surveyed labels which technique was performed, not how wrong it was. The gate is calibrated on injected faults with known truth, not on referees.
  • Blind spots, declared: stance width, hand shape, foot pivot, tempo/kime, chamber depth.
